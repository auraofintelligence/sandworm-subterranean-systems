Chapter 08 / 17 · Solar roads & paths

What if our roads and paths worked like LEGO?

Replaceable paving that can collect rain, carry services and, where it makes sense, generate solar electricity.

A road made of replaceable pieces

Think of a paved path made from interlocking pieces. If a section fails, replace that piece instead of resurfacing the whole road. Some pieces could make solar electricity, some could carry services and others could collect water.

Make

Use suitable recovered sand.

Lay

Explore robots fitting pieces together.

Use

Walking, riding, power and water.

Renew

Inspect, repair and reuse pieces.

Luke's earlier thought experiment was inspired by hexagonal solar-road prototypes. Sandworm would explore the whole system, not just putting solar panels in a road.

Other people have already given this a go

Scott and Julie Brusaw developed hexagonal Solar Roadways panels in the USA, including a 2014 prototype. The Dutch SolaRoad cycleway, also opened in 2014, reportedly generated about 73 kWh per square metre in its first year, but faced coating and light-transmission problems. Those are useful lessons, not proof that every road should be solar.

What could the pieces do?

Fit together

Replace a damaged component instead of the entire surface.

Make power

Compare solar paving with rooftop and canopy solar in real shade and dirt.

Store or share energy

Support lighting, charging, local power and suitable heat storage.

Manage rain

Collect, filter and direct runoff while protecting groundwater.

Support transport

Explore safe local autonomous vehicles without forcing them to rely on electronics in the pavement.

Robot maintenance

Explore machines that lay, inspect, clean and swap pieces.

Start with a path, not a highway

Footpaths and bike paths carry lighter loads than roads, but still need good wet grip, smooth surfaces and wheelchair access. Controlled tests are a sensible place to learn.

Test glass, ceramics, bound sand and other materials. Measure grip, heat, wear, fire safety, water movement, energy output and repair costs. Solar paving has to earn its place alongside simpler solar and conventional paving.

Where could it help locally?

Gumpi entrances, park-and-ride areas and paths could be places to explore modular surfaces. SMBI could compare road, drainage and footpath replacement over time. New parts could be made and maintained by local businesses and robots.

A digital record could tell us what a piece is made from, when it was installed and when it needs attention.
