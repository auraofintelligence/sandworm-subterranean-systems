Chapter 08 / 17 · Solar roads & paths

Paving that generates, drains and renews

A long-standing thought experiment: use recovered sand to develop interlocking, replaceable paving for roads, walking paths and cycleways, with solar generation, energy storage, water management and autonomous maintenance.

An old thought experiment with a new systems context

Years ago, Luke Nathan Hayes began imagining a modular road system inspired by the hexagonal solar roadway prototypes publicised in the early 2010s. Sandworm adds a potential local source of manufacturing feedstock, autonomous construction and maintenance, regional transport, stormwater management and community energy infrastructure to that thought experiment.

The vision extends equally to paved footpaths, walking trails, cycleways and shared paths. Rather than reproduce an existing panel or prescribe a single construction method, rethink the entire paved corridor as an adaptable collection of interlocking, individually replaceable components.

Historical inspiration: hexagonal solar road panels

Early hexagonal Solar Roadways panels and the Dutch SolaRoad cycleway are useful experimental precedents.

Read the research detail

Scott and Julie Brusaw's Solar Roadways received US Federal Highway Administration research support in 2009 and follow-up funding in 2011. Its second-generation SR2 prototype used hexagonal panels in a proof-of-concept parking area, widely publicised in 2014. The hexagonal geometry was intended to improve handling and accommodate curves and hills. These were research projects, not proof of viability for every public road.

Another important precedent is the SolaRoad cycleway pilot at Krommenie in the Netherlands, opened in 2014. Its reported first-year yield was about 73 kWh per square metre, but the surface coating also experienced delamination and declining light transmission. Later experience reinforces the importance of surface durability and maintenance. The hexagonal prototype and the cycleway pilot are distinct projects.

From tunnel spoil to the paved network

Silica and heavy-mineral sands excavated by Sandworm could become feedstock for selected paving-system components, subject to lawful ownership, composition testing and manufacturing feasibility. Explore glass, ceramics, sintered or bound granular materials, geopolymer composites and other candidates through Extreme Matter Atlas. Photovoltaic-grade silicon is a separate, high-purity industrial value chain, not an automatic consequence of having silica sand.

Connect material processing and local manufacturing to Spoil and energy and Straddie Clean Energy Superpower. Compare embodied energy, water use, lifecycle emissions, durability, cost and recoverability against conventional materials.

Interlocking and replaceable

Explore tessellating geometry and interchangeable components that can be removed individually without rebuilding an entire road or path. No particular size, shape, joint or structural system is specified.

Power generation

Explore solar-active modules, conventional modules, solar canopies and adjacent solar installations. Not every square metre needs to generate electricity. Measure useful annual energy under real shade, dirt and traffic conditions.

Energy storage and distribution

Investigate shared electrical storage, protected power and data distribution, lighting, mobility charging and suitable sand-based thermal storage. Storage location and technology remain open.

Stormwater management

Collect, slow, filter and direct runoff through appropriate modules or separate drainage infrastructure. Protect aquifer lenses, treat polluted runoff and avoid worsening erosion or flooding.

Autonomous transport

Investigate how roads can support safe autonomous vehicle navigation, inspection and energy services without making driving dependent on proprietary electronics embedded in the road.

Robotic laying and maintenance

Explore autonomous material handling, laying, inspection, cleaning, retrieval, replacement and recycling. Keep each component traceable through its lifetime.

Each surface has different load, grip, accessibility and safety requirements.

Walking paths and cycleways may offer useful earlier research settings because they avoid ordinary heavy vehicle axle loading. They have their own stringent accessibility, grip, comfort and safety requirements. Begin with non-public controlled test areas before considering approved public pilots.

A road that can renew itself

Imagine locally produced components entering a stock of compatible replacements, being placed by robotic equipment, inspected during their service life and returned for repair or material recovery. The goal is an incrementally improvable network rather than repeated wholesale repaving.

Use the Web3 sensorium and local compute to investigate digital inventories, simulation, condition monitoring, robotic task planning and maintenance histories. Safety-critical control must not depend on blockchain or remote AI availability. Connect supervised robotic experimentation with ready SET Co-op and the Maker-Space Lab.

First principles, not premature specifications

Existing road and solar experiments reveal useful questions about traction, load, heat, optical transmission, shade, cleaning, abrasion, corrosion, fire, flooding, energy return and lifecycle costs. They do not dictate the future design. Explore the full architecture before choosing how its functions are distributed across the paving and the surrounding corridor.

Benchmark each option against conventional paving with independent rooftop or canopy solar, drainage and storage. A system can create public value through easier maintenance, water management or local manufacturing even where embedded photovoltaics do not earn their place. This is pre-specification research, not a claim of feasibility, a construction plan or an approved project.

Where it fits into the local business case

For SMBI, compare the whole-life costs of roads, footpaths, cycleways, drainage and electricity infrastructure within the broader infrastructure backlog. For Gumpi, explore accessible pedestrian connections, cycle access, interchanges and paving renewal as distinct options in the future business case. Do not claim savings or redirect existing funding before independently comparable evidence is available.

A programmable physical infrastructure network

Explore next-generation materials, modular generation and storage, water management, interoperable components, autonomous installation and robotic renewal across roads, walkways and cycleways. The aim is continuous upgradeability rather than merely adding photovoltaic panels to existing pavement.

Manufacturing and energy integration

Treat the paving portfolio as a potential outlet for excavated sand and a component of the larger solar, gravity, thermal and electrical storage system. Compare energy output, runoff, repairability and material recovery, with robotically manufactured and replaceable service-bearing modules.
