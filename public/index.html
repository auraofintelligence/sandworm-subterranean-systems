Build below. Give the surface back.

A first-principles engineering venture for saturated mineral sands, shared underground services and a more resilient Moreton Bay.

Explore the SMBI costs →
This edition establishes the research questions, opportunities and public cost context. Engineering specifications, geometries, equipment selections and detailed system designs have not yet been developed.

The core Sandworm proposition

Build serviceable tunnels, not buried or seabed-laid pipes. The starting concept is a robot-accessible subterranean corridor approximately 1 to 1.5 metres in diameter, including potential crossings beneath the bay floor. Water, wastewater, electrical and communications infrastructure can be installed, inspected, serviced and renewed inside the corridor rather than requiring a new excavation for every service or replacement.

The diameter range is the project's initial research target, not a certified internal clearance or a final excavation diameter. The geometry, service segregation, lining, access and robotic requirements will be developed together from first principles.

A frontier engineering enterprise for the whole archipelago

Minjerribah (North Stradbroke), Mulgumpin (Moreton), Bribie, South Stradbroke, Coochiemudlo, Russell, Macleay, Lamb, Karragarra and the other islands of Moreton Bay and the adjoining Broadwater. Council and electoral boundaries do not limit the network. Each island has its own existing assets, service needs, ecological context and decision rights. Research spans established networks, independent utilities and small-scale infrastructure; it does not assume any island is infrastructure-free.

Sandworm is not a conventional civil contractor with a few robots added. The goal is to co-design novel materials, autonomous construction machinery, robotic serviceability, AI simulation, local manufacturing and community-owned productive capital as one evolving technological stack.

Gumpi Town Tunnel Loop

The proposed first Sandworm project on Minjerribah is the Gumpi Town Tunnel Loop, connecting the main town destinations and park-and-ride facilities while retaining regular passenger ferry services at both One Mile and Junner Street. Explore the first-project proposal.

Start with useful public services and local skills, not a promise of an instant subterranean city.

The governing idea

Existing engineering is an evidence library, not the limit of human imagination. Neither a textbook nor an attractive rendering is proof of what can or cannot be built. Every proposed departure earns its place through measurement, replication, risk control and independently reviewable evidence.
