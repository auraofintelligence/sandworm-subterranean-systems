Chapter 04 / 17 · Wet-sand engineering

Start with the grain, not the machine

The initial Sandworm design domain is saturated silica and heavy mineral sands, including aquifer lenses and submerged settings, not a universal hard-rock machine.

The first-principles stack

Granular mechanics

Characterise grading, packing, shear response, permeability, abrasion and mineral variability. Treat grain-scale and continuum descriptions as complementary.

Hydrogeology

Map freshwater and saline interfaces, pore pressure, flow paths, perched lenses and recharge. Groundwater protection is a design constraint, not an afterthought.

Mechanical architecture

Compare pressure-balanced excavation, slurry transport, sealed tooling, modular supports and inspection strategies against measured ground conditions.

Energy and wear

Account for cutting, circulation, pumps, separation, electronics, consumables, wear particles, heat rejection and maintenance. Measure energy per accepted volume.

Lining and junctions

Investigate strength, permeability, joint reliability, corrosion, deformation, buoyancy and long-term material ageing.

Verification

Model uncertainty, validate instrument readings against physical specimens, reproduce results and record failures openly.

Aquifer and submerged environments

Seqwater identifies Minjerribah groundwater as a critical source for local towns. Sub-bay or sub-ocean routes introduce hydraulic and ecological boundaries as well as construction constraints. The first laboratory test must not disturb a live aquifer, wetland, seabed or community water supply.

Seqwater water security programme ↗

No assumption is made that any proposed alignment is suitable for tunnelling. Site-specific geology may rule out a route or require a different architecture. The research question remains open.

What counts as a breakthrough

A repeatable advance in water isolation, excavation energy, containment, wear, lining durability or maintainability, with measured error bars and a comparison against the best available baseline. A negative result is valuable when it rules out an expensive direction.

Design around serviceability

The initial utility tunnel research target is approximately 1 to 1.5 metres diameter. The objective is enough internal space for separately contained services and robotic access, including inspection, manipulation, component removal and recovery. Determine the relationship between excavated diameter, finished internal diameter, lining, service occupancy, clearance and maintenance envelope through future engineering research. No particular cross-section or robot design is selected yet.

Compare land and sub-seabed conditions and evaluate pressure, seepage, structural integrity, access, service segregation and failure containment together. The tunnel must remain a maintainable asset rather than becoming inaccessible once the utilities are installed.

Redesign the entire engineering stack

Explore AI-guided materials discovery, multiphysics simulation, autonomous excavation, sensing, fabrication, adaptive control and robot-serviceable infrastructure as coupled research domains. Existing methods supply comparative evidence, not a ceiling on the technology. No mechanism or material has been selected.

Gumpi Town Tunnel Loop

The proposed Gumpi Town Tunnel Loop introduces a separate passenger-scale engineering challenge. Do not apply the 1 to 1.5 metre utility corridor research target to passenger transport; future vehicle, access and safety requirements will determine that architecture.
