Chapter 11 / 17 · Compute & sensorium

Give the underground its own nervous system

Sensors, local AI, digital twins and robots working together to make infrastructure easier to understand and look after.

Think of the underground as a living system

Our bodies have nerves that tell us when something hurts. Sandworm's sensorium would do something similar for infrastructure: sensors tell us what is happening, digital models help us understand it, and robots help us act on it.

Sense

Measure water, pressure, temperature and equipment condition.

Understand

Build a digital copy of the real infrastructure.

Act

Send robots to inspect or repair.

Check

Keep a trustworthy record of what happened.

What technology would make it work?

Digital twins

Computer models updated using measurements from the real thing. Test an idea in the model before trying it underground.

Local AI

Run simulations, look for unusual readings and train autonomous machines using nearby computers.

Web3 records

Use signed records and cryptographic checks to track changes, inspections and component history. Keep private information protected.

Local safety controls

Essential protection must keep working without an internet connection, AI service or blockchain.

Robots that look after the pipes and cables

A robot could inspect a valve, locate a fault, replace a component or carry a part through a utility tunnel. Work out whether it can fit, turn, reach, recover from a fault and get back out. Give equipment a digital record of its age, location, repairs and test results.

Bring useful computing power to the islands

The Installing the Missing Middle project proposes distributed community AI computing, with a national planning target of one rack-equivalent per 10,000 people. Sandworm could use that computing for design, simulation and robots. Computers may be above or below ground depending on power, cooling, flooding and maintenance.

The Minjerribah Living Twin supplies the wider idea: a shared digital understanding of the island. Sensitive infrastructure and cultural information need appropriate access controls.

Connect the whole system

The same records could track each load of excavated sand, manufactured parts, electricity production, batteries, water use, underground passenger and goods vehicles, and maintenance. Cultural access permissions remain under the authority of the relevant custodians, not an AI.

Start with invented test data before collecting sensitive real-world readings.
