Chapter 11 / 17 · Compute & sensorium

Give the underground a nervous system

A local AI modelling and sensing platform designed around reliability, provenance and community ownership.

A layered Web3 sensorium

From the physical world to accountable intelligence

  1. SenseCalibrated telemetry
  2. ModelDigital twins and simulation
  3. ActSupervised autonomous systems
  4. VerifySigned records and review

Physical sensing

Water pressure, temperature, deformation, vibration, chemistry, electrical health and equipment condition. Store calibration, uncertainty and sensor identity.

Local control

Keep safety-critical protection deterministic, isolated and operable without an AI model, blockchain, internet or central cloud.

Digital twin

Combine geospatial geometry, geology, utilities, component history, model assumptions and live telemetry. Replay events and simulate alternative designs.

Distributed AI

Use local compute for modelling, maintenance triage, anomaly detection and training simulation. Human sign-off remains necessary for consequential decisions.

Web3 audit

Cryptographic signatures and hashes can anchor inspections, model versions and provenance. Store raw sensitive data off-chain; use role-based access and revocation.

Data governance

Respect cultural and infrastructure-sensitive information, worker privacy, network security and data retention. Separate public dashboards from operational controls.

Installing the Missing Middle

The companion programme proposes one community rack-equivalent per 10,000 residents as a national planning target. Sandworm can supply research workloads for local modelling and digital twins, without assuming that racks themselves must be underground. Evaluate power, cooling, flood exposure and maintenance first. Installing the Missing Middle ↗.

Minjerribah Living Twin ↗ provides the regional spatial context. Use synthetic datasets before collecting restricted real-world telemetry.

Marine robotics and space analogues

Apply this research stream to robotic aquaculture and modular construction, with separate marine costs, permissions, environmental limits and off-world qualification requirements.

Modular solar roads and paths

Explore interlocking paving for roads, walking paths and cycleways as a potential recovered-sand manufacturing, stormwater, energy and autonomous maintenance research programme.

Robots that service the services

Develop digital twins and simulation environments around the proposed 1 to 1.5 metre diameter utility corridor. Explore robotic inspection, fault localisation, cleaning, manipulation, repair, replacement, component transport and robot recovery. Model available workspace alongside utility occupancy rather than assuming the full tunnel diameter remains clear. Give each service component a traceable identity and maintenance history. Robot capabilities and safe intervention methods remain to be demonstrated.

An autonomous engineering sensorium

Explore interoperable digital twins, edge intelligence, multi-robot coordination, continuous condition assessment, versioned simulation and independently verifiable operational records. Co-design the data layer with the physical architecture instead of attaching sensors after construction.

Measure the coupled system

Integrate excavation-volume ledgers, material passports, fabrication throughput, demand, energy generation and storage, maintenance and water systems into the shared digital twin. Each value requires provenance, uncertainty and appropriate access controls.

Explore the material and energy programme.

Coordinate a modular underground fleet

Research interoperable fleet coordination, passenger and goods demand, charging, condition monitoring and permissioned destination access. Cultural permissions remain under the relevant custodians' authority, with privacy and restricted data protected.
