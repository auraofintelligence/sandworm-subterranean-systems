Chapter 09 / 17 · Disaster resilience

Be ready when the weather turns

Protect the services islanders depend on and explore new ways to repair damage and recover after an emergency.

What needs to keep working when a storm hits?

When the power goes off, roads flood or boats stop running, island life gets complicated very quickly. Sandworm explores how protected services, backup energy and robots might help get the essentials working again.

Storms and floods

Keep water, electricity and communications available where possible, and isolate damaged equipment.

Long outages

Use distributed power, spare parts, water treatment and communications, with essential systems independent of the internet.

After the event

Robots could inspect damage, help repair services and move supplies once it is safe.

Know what's happening

Sensors and local models could show where faults are and what needs attention first.

Could underground rooms help in an emergency?

Protected spaces are worth researching, but an underground room can be especially dangerous during a tsunami or flood if water enters or exits are blocked. Before anyone could use a refuge, we would need to prove safe access, ventilation, fire protection, backup power, water exclusion and independent escape routes.

For a real emergency, follow official evacuation advice, including moving to higher ground when directed. No Sandworm tunnel is currently a public shelter.

How do we know it is helping?

Compare how quickly services return, how many people retain clean water and communications, repair costs, safety and environmental damage. Compare with elevated equipment, mobile systems and other alternatives. An all-weather walking route in ordinary rain is a different question from keeping it open in an extreme event.
