Chapter 05 / 15 · Lateral sand systems

From a tunnel to a configurable volume

Study whether controlled lateral excavation from a stable service spine could support useful underground infrastructure without treating wet sand as self-supporting.

Why lateral geometry matters

A narrow trunk corridor might be useful for utilities but a wider local chamber could enable pump equipment, maintenance, distributed compute or logistics. In saturated unconsolidated sand, excavation changes load paths and pore-water conditions. A tunnel wall is not a free mining face.

Research questions

Geometry

Compare discrete pods, parallel bores, modular junctions and larger structures by volume, surface footprint and maintainability.

Isolation

Explore whether separate containment boundaries and redundant barriers protect water quality and prevent a local failure propagating through the network.

Material formation

Evaluate prefabricated or in-situ material concepts through controlled material tests. Establish how strength and permeability change with time.

Whole-life access

Compare remotely replaceable modules, isolated service bays, inspectable compartments and independent recovery routes.

Evidence before people

Start with non-occupied sealed experimental cells at laboratory scale. Progress only through independent geotechnical review, containment evidence, design certification and consent. Occupied rooms, emergency use and cross-water links are separate approval classes. No dimensions, alignment or construction sequence are represented as a design.

Upward expansion into the sea

Explore robotic aquaculture polyps as a distinct, consent-led research application of modular geometry, with removable seabed structures and separate marine approvals.
