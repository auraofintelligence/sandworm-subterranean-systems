Chapter 02 / 17 · SMBI economics

The billion-dollar question

Russell, Macleay, Lamb and Karragarra need durable wastewater and utility solutions that households can afford.

What is publicly known

Redland City Council states that SMBI has no council reticulated wastewater network and properties require their own effluent systems. In 2026 Councillor Shane Rendalls published an independent indicative sewerage model, not an adopted council budget or a peer-reviewed feasibility study.

Compare service systems rather than just construction methods

Maintain and improve onsite

Establish the true household replacement, maintenance and water-quality baseline; consider precinct-scale treatment where appropriate.

Conventional reticulation

Compare open trench, pressure sewer, vacuum sewer and trenchless crossings with treatment capacity and road restoration.

Sandworm utility spine

Investigate approximately 1 to 1.5 metre diameter robot-serviceable utility tunnels beneath land and the bay floor, with separate service containment and internal access for inspection, maintenance and replacement.

Hybrid staged delivery

Prioritise high-demand precincts and replace or extend the network only where the benefit exceeds whole-of-life cost.

Water, power and communications

Wastewater is only one service. Seqwater identifies the bulk island supply as dependent on a single Russell Island offtake and has undertaken Macleay pipeline replacement. Obtain asset-specific contract values, age, failures, renewals, service interruptions and actual route lengths before claiming joint savings. Do not allocate the entire sewerage estimate to tunnelling.

Research comparison, not a quantified cost saving.

The existing underwater water pipeline expenditure is a reference for the cost of today's infrastructure, not the Sandworm construction method. Sandworm proposes approximately 1 to 1.5 metre diameter tunnels beneath the seabed and beneath island land, with usable internal access for robots and separately contained utility systems. The business case must compare the higher initial investment in an accessible corridor against the combined whole-life cost of multiple conventional services, including repeated excavation, marine works, failures and renewals.

Investigate drinking-water lines, wastewater lines, power, fibre and environmental sensing. Compatibility, independent containment, usable robotic clearance, fault isolation, ventilation or other internal environmental conditions, drainage, retrieval and maintenance remain research questions. Not every service necessarily belongs in the same tunnel.

A starting comparator, not a regional limit

Use the SMBI costs as a starting point for developing the wider archipelago asset and lifecycle cost inventory. Compare frontier robot-accessible utility corridors, autonomous asset servicing and modular upgrades against existing infrastructure on every relevant island.
