Chapter 02 / 17 · SMBI economics

Could we make island services easier to fix?

Before spending a billion dollars on sewerage, let's ask what sort of infrastructure the Southern Moreton Bay Islands could get for their money.

What if we could stop digging up the same ground?

Russell, Macleay, Lamb and Karragarra already have water, electricity, roads and ferries. But homes rely on their own wastewater systems because there is no council sewerage network. Sandworm asks whether the next round of infrastructure spending could leave behind a permanent passage for robots to look after pipes and cables.

Two approaches to underground services

  1. TodayInstall pipes and cables separately.
  2. When something breaksFind a way to reach the buried or underwater service.
  3. Sandworm's ideaBuild a passage with room for service robots.
  4. Future repairsReach and replace components from inside.

A tunnel may cost more to build. The question is whether that extra cost pays off through easier repairs, upgrades and replacements over its working life.

A real repair bill from the bay

A leaking underwater water main between Lamb and Macleay led to a 400-metre replacement. It shows why reaching services is a cost worth exploring.

Four ways forward

Improve existing systems

Find out what households spend on maintaining and replacing their wastewater systems.

Conventional sewerage

Compare trenches, pressure pipes, vacuum systems, treatment and putting roads back together.

Sandworm utility tunnels

Explore a shared passage roughly 1 to 1.5 metres across, for appropriately separated pipes, cables and robots.

A bit of both

Start where the need is greatest and use the best approach for each place.

Drinking water, sewerage, electricity and internet connections have different safety needs. Not every service will belong in the same tunnel.

What we still need to find out

Seqwater identifies a single Russell Island connection for the bulk island water supply. We need the actual costs and maintenance records for water, power, communications and household wastewater. The roads, drainage and parking backlog is a separate issue, not another sewerage bill.
