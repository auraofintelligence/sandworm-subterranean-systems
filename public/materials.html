Could we invent better building materials?

Use first-principles thinking, AI and practical experiments to go beyond the materials already available.

Sand is not just sand

Look closely at a handful of island sand and you can see different grains. At the smallest scales, their composition and structure influence how materials behave. What if we could design materials around the problems of working underground: water, pressure, wear, heat and repairs?

The Extreme Matter Atlas looks at materials from atoms and crystal structures through to speculative designs. Sandworm would use it to explore, simulate and test new ideas.

What could we invent?

Stronger, repairable materials

Explore grains, binders and reinforcement that resist water and can be repaired.

Metamaterials

Materials whose shape or internal pattern gives them interesting properties, such as controlling vibration, sound or heat.

Materials that report changes

Investigate electrical, optical, magnetic or acoustic sensing built into components.

New ways of making things

Use AI, simulation and automated experiments to search for useful recipes and ways to make them.

Ask what is possible, then test it

Imagine

What would a useful material do?

Model

Work out how it might behave.

Test

Make samples and measure them.

Repeat

Check that the results hold up.

Existing science can guide experiments without being treated as a limit on imagination. A prediction or an attractive render is not proof that a material works. Record failures as well as successes.

Give useful materials somewhere to go

Quick-fit blocks, tunnel linings, glass, ceramics, solar paths, gravity weights, heat stores and marine structures are all potential applications. Each has different strength, durability, safety, energy and cost requirements.
