Licence And Reuse

How people can read, question, fork, create their own repo, adapt the builders and generate Markdown while keeping provenance, safety and authority clear.

Public infrastructure

What reuse helps the work stay useful and honest?

Sandworm is shared as a public-interest workbench. People can read it, question it, use the builders, make their own Markdown notes, and fork or copy the repo as a scaffold for their own repo while keeping the source trail visible.

Builder output

Who owns a generated `.md`?

The person who writes the answers controls their downloaded Markdown. The site does not need to host it, approve it or claim it.

Code

Could the public repo be copied?

Yes, for public-interest, educational, community, artistic, regenerative or review purposes, if attribution, licence notes and honest provenance stay visible.

Own repo

What makes a fork honest?

A new repo should name itself clearly, avoid implying endorsement, keep links back to the original, and show what has changed.

Story material

What stays protected?

The Sandworm name, writings, images, narrative materials, brand assets and public story world are not offered as raw material to sell, mislabel or repackage.

AI use

Can AI tools use the Markdown?

Yes, as context for drafts, letters, critique, lessons or expressions of interest, while keeping facts, sources, privacy, cultural authority and review status visible.

Reality check

What is not granted?

No page, builder, fork or AI output is engineering approval, legal advice, cultural permission, environmental approval, financial advice or official endorsement.

Plain path

Could someone build their own repo without muddying the source?

A respectful fork can be simple: copy the public repo, keep the original licence and source links, name the new repo for the visionary, engineering, group or review path, change the pages and builders, then add notes showing what is draft, sourced, reviewed or still uncertain.

Good reuse leaves a trail. People can see what came from Sandworm, what changed, and who now carries the local responsibility.
